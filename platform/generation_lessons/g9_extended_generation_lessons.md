LESSONS FROM MARKING -- Grade 9 Extended

These come from marking real Grade 9 Extended scripts. They apply to every paper, packet, answer sketch and mark scheme you write for this course, on top of the rules above.

The mark scheme
- Name the wrong routes students actually take, with what each earns, so a marker never has to guess: making x the subject by dividing by one coefficient without factoring (M1M0A0); taking two percentage changes as a product or sum of the rates rather than of the multipliers (M0A0); reasoning about the wrong value in a domain question (R0).
- Wherever a part asks for a condition, a value or a conclusion that follows from an earlier part, say what a blank or unsupported answer earns, and state the follow-through: from which earlier result, and whether a wrong or unfactored earlier answer still allows it.
- For an "explain why" part, list every accepted route and what does not count: a restated verdict, a restated question, or a numerical check built on a wrong model.
- Where the form of the final answer matters (a complete factorisation, a simplified value), say which form earns the A mark.

The page
- Give every part its own labelled space, with its own Answer line where one is printed, and keep the working spaces of neighbouring parts visibly apart.
- Tell students in the instructions to cross out work rather than rub it out, and to box or underline their final answer.
- Do not name variables with letters that look like digits in handwriting: b (6), l (1), O and o (0), S (5), Z and z (2), g and q (9). Prefer a, c, k, m, n, p, r, t, x and y.
- Size each working space to the work it has to hold, so working does not spill into the margins or into a neighbouring part.
