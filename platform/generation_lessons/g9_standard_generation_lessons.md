LESSONS FROM MARKING -- Grade 9 Standard

These come from marking real Grade 9 scripts. They apply to every packet, answer sketch and piece of marking guidance you write for this course, on top of the rules above. Grade 9 Standard is assessed against strand descriptors (Exceeding, Meeting, Approaching, Beginning), not mark codes: write marking guidance as what a response shows at each level, never as M1, A1 or R1.

Marking guidance
- Name the wrong routes students actually take and the evidence each shows, so a marker never has to guess: making x the subject by dividing by one coefficient without factoring shows a start on the method but not the method; taking two percentage changes as a product or sum of the rates, rather than of the multipliers, does not show the idea of successive change.
- Wherever a part asks for a condition, a value or a conclusion that follows from an earlier part, say what a blank or unsupported answer shows, and whether work carried forward from a wrong earlier answer still counts as evidence.
- For an "explain why" part, describe what a convincing explanation contains and what does not count: a restated verdict, a restated question, or a numerical check built on a wrong model.

The page
- Give every part its own labelled space, with its own answer line where one is printed, and keep the working spaces of neighbouring parts visibly apart.
- Tell students in the instructions to cross out work rather than rub it out, and to box or underline their final answer.
- Do not name variables with letters that look like digits in handwriting: b (6), l (1), O and o (0), S (5), Z and z (2), g and q (9). Prefer a, c, k, m, n, p, r, t, x and y.
- Size each working space to the work it has to hold, so working does not spill into the margins or into a neighbouring part.
